Launch day is the worst time to judge a crowdfunding campaign — everything is promise, nothing is proof. So we went back to the live campaign pages the morning after, checked actual funding against the PR, read the delivery fine print, and looked at what backers are saying in the comments.

Six campaigns survived the check. Five earned a back verdict, each with the honest trade-offs attached. One earned a warning instead. Every figure below was verified against the live campaign page on October 14, 2026.

HOZO Hobby Knife — the launch-day runaway

Back — the safest back of the week.

A modular hobby-knife system whose storage case unfolds into a cutting workstation.

What it does

Push-button blade changes (no more fumbling with tiny screws), a blade magazine that holds everything point-down with magnets, and a one-way disposal slot for dead blades. Blade set covers cutting, carving, sawing and filing: standard 30° blade, long blade, chisel and mini-chisel, curved, double-edge, U and V gouges, a saw and a metal file. This is HOZO's follow-up to its NeoSander and NeoBlade tools — a brand extension, not a debut.

Who it's for: scale and Gundam modelers, 3D-print cleanup, leathercraft, jewelry making, scrapbooking — anyone whose current blade storage is a drawer of loose sharps.

Spiny 2 — the clip-on e-bike kit, second try

Back — gen-two plus press coverage is the trust signal.

A 2.2 kg friction-drive motor that clips onto your regular bike and turns it electric in seconds — then comes off when you park.

What it does

Cadence and speed sensors detect your pedaling and engage the rear tyre in about 0.25 seconds, with five assist levels. When you stop pedaling, the motor lifts away and the bike rolls unassisted. USB-C PD charging, Bluetooth LE, IP65 weather rating. This is the second generation, rebuilt from feedback by roughly 500 first-gen riders across Europe.

Who it's for: bike owners who want e-assist for the commute without buying a full e-bike — or worrying about locking one up.

Elevated Craft Complete Cocktail System — the safe pair of hands

Back — on the best fulfillment record in this roundup.

A modular, design-matched home bar system — shaker plus prep tools conceived as one set instead of accumulated piecemeal.

What it does

Takes the brand's leak-proof, vacuum-insulated shaker platform and extends it into a full system: measuring, muddling, stirring and straining tools that share the same design language and live together instead of rattling around a drawer. Three-plus years in development; the company's fourth crowdfunding campaign.

Who it's for: home-bartending enthusiasts who already make cocktails weekly and are tired of a bar setup assembled from five different brands.

Benjamin — real buttons for YouTube music lessons

Back — the most focused product in this roundup.

A tactile control surface and adjustable iPad stand that gives musicians physical loop, speed and volume controls for video-based practice.

What it does

Tap IN and OUT to loop any passage instantly, nudge boundaries frame by frame with a rotary wheel, and dial playback speed in 5% increments instead of YouTube's coarse jumps — all by touch, without looking away from your instrument. A bank of tactile backlit switches covers play/pause, mute, fullscreen, captions and bookmarks; a physical slider handles volume. The companion app saves named loops and playlists. Plays YouTube in-app, works with Apple Music and local media files, mounts on tripods and VESA arms.

Who it's for: musicians — guitarists, pianists, drummers — who learn from YouTube tutorials and are tired of stopping mid-phrase to drag a tiny progress bar.

ComboX — the cleverest product, thinnest resume

Back with eyes open — best product thinking, thinnest resume.

A desktop macro console (10 hot-swappable keys, joystick, dial, LCD) with an AI Link feature that routes captured screen content to AI services.

What it does

The joystick switches virtual desktops and macOS Spaces physically; multi-action and combo-action macros collapse app-launching and workflow sequences into single presses; seven customizable layers adapt it per app. AI Link captures on-screen content and sends it to your configured AI model via a Chrome extension — plus a plugin store, Premiere Pro/After Effects/OBS integrations, a mobile mode for tablets and phones, and a Claude Code plugin with voice control.

Who it's for: streamers, video editors, developers and AI-power-users who live in keyboard shortcuts and want them under their fingers instead.

FIREUP Everyday Braiser — watch, don't back

Watch, don't back — the campaign sequencing is the problem.

A cast-iron braiser with Oxford-patented heat-exchange fins, claimed to heat 44% faster.

What it does

Wide, shallow cast-iron walls with fin technology adapted from rocket heat exchangers, meant to spread heat across the base and up the sides for faster, more even cooking — sear, braise, roast, and serve from hob to oven to table.

Who it's for: home cooks who braise weekly and already own (and like) cast iron.

Frequently asked questions

What do your "Back", "Back with eyes open" and "Watch, don't back" verdicts actually mean?

"Back" is a straightforward recommendation — five of the six campaigns here earned one. "Back with eyes open" (ComboX) means the product thinking is the strongest in this roundup but the creator resume is the thinnest: Electrocookie's first product, just 47 backers, and a December 2026 delivery estimate that's aggressive for a first hardware run. "Watch, don't back" (FIREUP) means don't pledge — the problem is the campaign sequencing, not the product. Revisit if the Indiegogo orders ship.

Why is the FIREUP braiser a "watch, don't back" when it's 715% funded?

The funding isn't the problem — £54,058 pledged of a £7,558 goal from 370 backers is real traction. The problem is sequencing: FIREUP ran an Indiegogo "test campaign" for this same braiser, and Indiegogo backers report their orders still unshipped while the Kickstarter campaign runs at a lower price. The company's reply calls it a "smaller test campaign" ahead of the "full launch" — without addressing the unshipped orders. Taking new money for a product before fulfilling old orders is exactly the red-flag pattern in our crowdfunding red flags guide. The pan may be excellent; the campaign sequencing is not.

Which campaign ends soonest — is there one I need to decide on quickly?

Of the five back verdicts, Elevated Craft ends October 30 — the shortest fuse in this roundup, with no time to watch and wait. FIREUP ends October 29 but it's watch-only. The rest run longer: Spiny 2 ends November 11, the HOZO Hobby Knife and Benjamin end November 12, and ComboX ends November 19.

Why is there no verdict on the four "launching soon" products?

They're pre-launch, so there are no live campaign pages to verify against — and no back recommendations.
