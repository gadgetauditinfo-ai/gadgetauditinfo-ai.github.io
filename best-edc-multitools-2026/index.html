YSMART TOOLT — “The key-fob toolkit”

Best new crowdfunding pick

Thirty-six claimed functions and a replaceable utility blade fit inside a 7cm body built around three locking tool plates.

What it does / key features

TOOLT is the most unusual design here. Its three plates fan out and lock, creating working leverage from a body roughly the size of a car key fob. Plate one carries the wrenches, drivers, wire tools, saw, awl and bottle opener. Plate two adds the pry bar, scraper, tire lever, can opener and flathead. Plate three handles rulers, a Phillips driver and coarse and fine files. The separate Stanley-pattern utility blade is replaceable instead of becoming a permanent dull edge.

Who it is forPeople who want maximum function density and accept crowdfunding risk.
Campaign realityShipping is targeted for January 2027 if the project succeeds; it is not a retail delivery guarantee.

Verified specifications

Body70 × 32 × 18mm; 180mm fully deployed
Weight80g aluminum / ≈95g titanium
Tool systemThree locking plates; 36 claimed functions
BladeReplaceable Stanley 11-921 utility blade; two positions plus safety lock
Campaign priceEarly-bird tiers reported around $106 aluminum / $133 titanium
Pros and cons

Pros

  • A new blade-meets-toolbox layout
  • Replaceable commodity blade avoids permanent dullness
  • Locking plates make the small body usable

Cons

  • The 36-tool count includes generous items such as a Morse-code reference and shopping-cart key
  • Crowdfunding delivery risk remains
  • Titanium tier is expensive
Verdict

Back only if the compact mechanism matters enough to accept a January 2027 delivery target. The aluminum version makes more sense than paying heavily for titanium.

MetMo Pocket Grip — “The pocket machinist”

Best precision grip

A century-old parallel-wrench idea, squeezed into a pocket clamp that grips flat and awkward hardware without the jaws splaying apart.

What it does / key features

MetMo built the Pocket Grip around a 113-year-old parallel-wrench patent. The jaws stay parallel as they close, so force lands evenly on bolts, stripped fasteners and delicate work. It also works as pliers, a T-handle or inline hex driver, a small vice, and a tap holder; only the stainless version adds wire snips. The mechanism is overbuilt for a pocket tool and priced accordingly.

Who it is forModel makers, repairers and anyone who values controlled clamping over a long list of blades.
Why it stands outIt can act as a third hand across roughly 20–100mm workpieces.

Verified specifications

Jaws17-4 PH hardened stainless steel jaws (45 HRC per HiConsumption's spec sheet)

Price£109 aluminum / £164 stainless / £219 titanium (aluminum/Black pre-order, shipping wave February 2027)

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Parallel jaws grip awkward and partially rounded hardware
  • Useful as a compact clamp or third hand
  • 200-year guarantee

Cons

  • Very expensive for a pocket tool
  • Heavier than wallet tools
  • Aluminum and titanium versions have no cutting edge
Verdict

Buy it for the parallel-jaw mechanism, not the tool count. The aluminum version is the sensible carry; stainless earns its extra weight only if the snips matter.

Lever Gear Toolcard Pro — “The wallet backup”

Best wallet multitool

For $19.95, this 1.5mm steel card gives a wallet a credible set of emergency drivers, wrenches, rulers and openers without sharp edges.

What it does / key features

The Toolcard Pro is a 420 stainless steel (HRC 48 hardness) card cut into wrench sizes, open-end slots, screwdrivers, rulers, square guides, a protractor, bottle and can openers, cord cutter, bit holder, pry bar and nail puller. Its claimed 40 functions count every wrench size separately, but the card remains useful because it carries flat and cannot fold closed on your fingers.

Who it is forTravelers and minimalists who want a backup tool that truly fits a wallet.
Carry detailThe optional snap-on clip can hold up to six cards.

KONDOR BLUE EDC Multi-Tool V2 — “The camera-rig specialist”

Best for camera rigs

A purpose-built driver for cages, tripods and plates, with eight standard bits, two torque positions and a bubble level.

What it does / key features

KONDOR BLUE designed this around the screws that keep film gear together. Five one-inch bits sit below a magnetic flip lid, while three two-inch bits ride in the end with spring-loaded retention. A top socket gives a screwdriver grip; a side socket gives more torque. Multiple bits can be loaded at once, and the bubble level helps square a camera, prop or small rig.

Who it is forCamera operators, assistants and creators who regularly adjust cages, plates and tripods.
Carry hardwarePocket clip and lanyard loop are built in.

Harnds Sidekick — “The knife-first carry”

Best budget knife-tool

A compact button-lock knife puts the blade first, then adds a bottle opener, strap cutter, window breaker and cash clip.

What it does / key features

The Sidekick is a knife-led multitool, not a pliers tool with a compromised blade. Its 1.65-inch edge opens one-handed and locks with a button. The exposed hook doubles as a bottle opener and strap cutter, while the tail adds a glass breaker and the rear clip can hold cash. The campaign offers a budget 9Cr18MoV/G10 build and a premium M390/carbon-fiber tier. Harnds has run at least one previous knife campaign on Kickstarter (per New Atlas), though we couldn't confirm whether those backers actually got their knives.

Klarus Horizon MT07 — “The full-size value”

Best full-size value

The only full-size pliers multitool in this list costs $33.95 and still includes replaceable tungsten cutters, scissors and dual locking.

What it does / key features

The MT07 is a traditional 20-in-1 built for work rather than pocket minimalism. CNC-machined pliers lead the package, backed by safety-tip scissors, a saw, screwdrivers, bottle opener and glass breaker. The wire cutters use replaceable K40 tungsten inserts, while liner and lockback systems secure the fold-out tools. A pocket clip and anti-shift latch help tame the size, but roughly 280g still makes this better for a bag or belt.

Who it is forBuyers who need proper pliers and cutters at the lowest price in this group.

Bottom line

The Lever Gear Toolcard Pro is the easiest recommendation because it costs $19.95, disappears in a wallet and covers real backup jobs. The MetMo Pocket Grip is the most capable specialist for precise gripping, while the Klarus MT07 is the value workhorse when full-size pliers matter. Creators should look straight at KONDOR BLUE; knife-first carriers get the most value from the $26 Harnds Sidekick tier. YSMART TOOLT is the most inventive design, but it remains a crowdfunding preorder.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best EDC multitool for most people?

The $19.95 Lever Gear Toolcard Pro is the safest low-cost recommendation for backup use. If you need pliers, choose the Klarus MT07 and accept its 280g weight.

Can these multitools go through airport security?

Lever Gear and KONDOR BLUE market their blade-free tools as TSA-compliant, but screening officers make the final decision. The YSMART and Harnds tools include blades and should not go in carry-on luggage.

Is a large tool count always better?

No. Manufacturers often count each wrench size, ruler or reference marking as a separate function. Judge the tool by the jobs you will use, grip comfort and leverage.

Are the Kickstarter products safe to buy?

Backing is not the same as retail shopping. TOOLT and Sidekick have stated shipping targets, but delays, design changes and non-delivery remain possible. Never pledge money you cannot afford to lose.
