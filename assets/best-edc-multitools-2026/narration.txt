Here are six E D C multitools that earn their pocket space in twenty twenty-six.

This is a research review, not a hands-on test. Gadget Audit compared official specifications, dimensions, materials, prices, warranties, and campaign details. The list favors tools with a clear job over inflated feature counts.

First is the YSMART TOOLT, the best new crowdfunding pick. It puts thirty-six claimed functions across three locking plates inside a body just seven centimeters long. One plate carries hex wrenches, bit drivers, wire tools, a saw, an awl, and a bottle opener. Another adds a pry bar, scraper, tire lever, can opener, and flathead screwdriver. The third handles rulers, a Phillips driver, and files. A replaceable Stanley utility blade means the main edge does not become a permanent dull spot. The aluminum version weighs eighty grams and the titanium version weighs ninety grams. Early campaign pricing was reported at about one hundred six dollars for aluminum and one hundred thirty-three dollars for titanium. The design is genuinely clever, but the tool count is generous, the titanium tier is expensive, and shipping is only targeted for January twenty twenty-seven. Back it only if the mechanism matters enough to accept crowdfunding risk.

Second is the MetMo Pocket Grip, the best precision grip. It revives a one-hundred-thirteen-year-old parallel-wrench patent in a tool measuring ninety-five point five by forty-five point five by ten millimeters. Unlike ordinary pliers, its jaws stay parallel as they close, helping it grip bolts, stripped fasteners, and delicate work evenly. It also works as a hex driver, small vice, tap holder, and third hand. The stainless version adds wire snips. The jaw opens twenty millimeters and MetMo reports more than twenty-one kilograms of clamping force and fifteen point six newton meters of achieved torque. Prices run from one hundred nine pounds for aluminum to two hundred nineteen pounds for titanium. Buy it for the unusual clamping mechanism, not for a long feature list. The aluminum version makes the most sense for daily carry.

Third is the Lever Gear Toolcard Pro, the best wallet multitool. This heat-treated stainless card is about one point five millimeters thick and weighs roughly forty grams. It combines metric and inch wrench sizes, open-end wrenches, bottle and can openers, screwdrivers, rulers, square guides, a protractor, cord cutter, quarter-inch bit holder, pry bar, and nail puller. The claimed forty tools count individual wrench sizes separately, but the card still earns its place because it carries flat and costs nineteen dollars and ninety-five cents. It cannot replace pliers or a comfortable full-size driver, yet it is the easiest low-cost recommendation for travel and backup use.

Fourth is the KONDOR BLUE E D C Multi-Tool Version Two, the creator pick. It is designed around the quarter-inch fasteners used on camera cages, plates, tripods, and film rigs. Five one-inch bits live below a magnetic flip lid, and three two-inch bits are stored in the end. A top socket works like a screwdriver, while a side socket adds leverage for more torque. The built-in bubble level is genuinely useful when a camera, prop, or small rig needs to be squared. It measures one hundred ten by thirty-six by eighteen point five millimeters and weighs one hundred fifty-nine grams with bits. At eighty-nine dollars, it is expensive and specialized, but that specialization is the point. Put it in a camera bag; do not buy it as a general-purpose multitool unless you regularly work on production gear.

Fifth is the Harnds Sidekick, the best budget knife-tool. This is a small folding knife first, with a bottle opener, strap cutter, window breaker, and cash clip built around it. The one point six five inch blade opens one-handed and uses a button lock. Overall length is four point five three inches, and weight is about sixty-eight grams. The standard campaign tier combines nine C R eighteen M O V steel with G ten at twenty-six dollars. A premium version uses M three ninety steel and carbon fiber at eighty-nine dollars and ninety-nine cents. The standard model is the value play. The premium steel is attractive to enthusiasts, but it costs more than this compact design needs. Shipping is targeted for December twenty twenty-six, and local knife-carry rules still apply.

Sixth is the Klarus Horizon M T zero seven, the best full-size value. It is a traditional twenty-in-one built around C N C machined pliers, with safety-tip scissors, a saw, screwdrivers, bottle opener, and glass breaker. Its wire cutters use replaceable K forty tungsten inserts, and the fold-out tools use dual locking. At about two hundred eighty grams, it belongs in a bag or on a belt rather than a minimalist pocket. The checked sale price was thirty-three dollars and ninety-five cents. That makes it the strongest value here when proper pliers and cutters matter more than weight.

The bottom line is simple. The Lever Gear Toolcard Pro is the safest recommendation for most people because it is cheap, flat, and useful as a backup. The MetMo Pocket Grip is the most capable specialist for controlled gripping. The Klarus M T zero seven is the value workhorse. Creators should choose KONDOR BLUE, while knife-first carriers get the best value from the standard Harnds Sidekick. YSMART TOOLT is the most inventive, but it remains a preorder. Match the format to the job you actually do, and the tool is far more likely to stay in your pocket.
