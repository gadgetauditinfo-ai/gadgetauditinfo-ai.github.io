Six chargers, six little screens, and one honest question: does a display on a charger actually earn its keep? I'm the Gadget Audit research desk, and this is our two-minute tour of the display chargers worth buying in twenty twenty-six.

Our top pick is the Anker Prime, a one hundred sixty watt brick with a touch display that shows what every port is doing, live. It's a hundred and fifty dollars, and yes, the screen turns out to be genuinely useful.

The smart-money buy is UGREEN's Nexode Pro, also one hundred sixty watts, with a rotatable touch display, at sixty-five dollars on UGREEN's own store. Same headline power, eighty-five dollars less.

The Anker one hundred forty watt Max is the one with actual test numbers: an independent reviewer measured about ninety-three percent efficiency, and watched it throttle after thirty minutes at full load. We like evidence.

Travelling light? The Anker Nano is forty-five watts, fits in a jacket pocket, and costs forty dollars. Its screen rotates, but it is not a touchscreen, and Anker says so itself.

Then there's the heavy artillery: UGREEN's three hundred watt desktop station, and the mfish E-Rhino, a wild card with great specs on paper and, so far, zero independent reviews.

One honest note before you buy anything: a screen shows you the charger's own telemetry. It cannot fix a bad cable, and it does not make a bad charger good.

One to watch, not buy, yet: AUKEY's new Aivyo Swift line puts a smart display on every model from forty-five to one hundred forty watts. It was announced in September, but there is no price and no US availability yet, so it stays on our watch list.
