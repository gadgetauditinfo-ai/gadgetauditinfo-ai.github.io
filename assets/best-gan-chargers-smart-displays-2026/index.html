For years the charger spec war was about one number: watts. In 2026 it moved onto the charger itself. The new fight is a little screen that shows you live per-port wattage, internal temperature, and the protocol your device actually negotiated — the stuff that used to require a USB power meter and a free afternoon.

Is it useful? Sometimes, genuinely. A display tells you the cable you grabbed from the drawer is a 60W cable, not the 240W one you thought it was. It tells you your laptop is sipping 12W because it's already at 94%, not because the brick is broken. And it tells you when a charger is cooking itself. Six of these display chargers are worth your money. Here's the honest ranking — and where the evidence is thin, we say so.

Research-based review — no hands-on claims. Specs verified from manufacturer pages and reputable press as of October 3, 2026. Prices and availability as observed — verify before you buy.

Anker Prime 160W (A2687) — “the flagship that makes the case”

Top pick · $149.99, shipping now

The charger that makes the screen argument — 160W across three USB-C ports, and a display you'll actually read.

What it does / key features

Plug in a laptop, a phone, and a tablet, and the AnkerSense View touch display shows you what each port is doing: live wattage per port, internal temperature, even the cable type the port negotiated with. One port delivers the full 140W PD 3.1 — 16-inch MacBook Pro territory — and the Bluetooth companion app adds AI Mode 2.0 plus per-port shutdown timers, so a port can stop feeding a device once it's charged instead of trickling all night. GaNPrime 2.0 and PowerIQ 5.0 do the power juggling, ActiveShield watches temperatures around the clock, and the package was a CES 2026 Innovation Awards honoree. It is, in short, the state of the art with a screen on it.

The screen honesty note

Every display in this roundup shows the charger's own telemetry: the wattage each port negotiated, the charger's internal temperature, and — on the better screens — the charging protocol in use. That's genuinely useful for spotting a weak cable or the wrong port.

What a screen cannot do: judge your cable beyond what the port negotiated, predict battery health, or make a mediocre charger good. And unless an independent test says otherwise — the Anker 140W Max's 93.1% efficiency measurement is the rare example — the wattage on the glass is the brand's own measurement of itself. Treat the readouts as indicative, not laboratory truth.

Frequently asked questions

Why would I want a screen on my charger?

For debugging, mostly: you can see what each device actually negotiated — the cable that's quietly capping you at 60W, the port that's only offering 12W, the brick that's running hot. Plus temperature at a glance. But a screen doesn't make a bad charger good; GaN quality and protocol support still matter more than the display.

Why are there no buy links?

Affiliate links are wired only when they're supplied and approved. Until then, the disabled “Purchase link coming soon” button is this site's placeholder — it never invents URLs. Prices shown are as observed on October 3, 2026; verify the live price before you buy.

Do I need PD 3.1 or 140W?

Only if you're charging a 14- or 16-inch laptop at full speed. Phones top out far below that, and 45–65W covers most people's entire kit. Buy 140W for a big laptop, not for a spec sheet.

How accurate are the wattage readouts?

They're the charger's own telemetry — treat them as indicative. Independent measurements, like the 93.1% efficiency test on the Anker 140W Max, are rare; where they exist, we've noted them.

GaN vs GaNPrime — what's the difference?

GaN is the transistor technology: gallium nitride switches faster than silicon, so chargers get smaller, cooler, and more efficient. GaNPrime is Anker's branding for its stack — GaN II silicon, PowerIQ 5.0 power management, and ActiveShield monitoring. It's a marketing layer on top of the technology, not a standard anyone else certifies.
