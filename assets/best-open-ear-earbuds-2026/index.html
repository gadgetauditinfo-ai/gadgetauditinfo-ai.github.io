Open-ear stopped being a curiosity in 2026. Bose moved from the original 2024 Ultra Open experiment to a structured two-product family; Shokz added flagship and value clip-ons in June, Anker added LDAC in September, 1MORE brought an $80 flagship-style specification sheet, and Baseus is attempting ANC in an unsealed design.

Six available or pre-order choices from $79.99 to $299, plus one preview that is not yet a recommendation. If you still prefer a sealed design and stronger isolation, start with our wireless earbuds under $100 guide.

Bose Ultra Open Earbuds 2nd Gen — “the premium reference”

The premium reference

A newly structured flagship for all-day listening, with Bose’s best codec support, spatial modes and a wireless-charging case.

What it does / key features

Bose says its new OpenAudio driver improves bass and maximum volume while keeping the ear canal unsealed. Cinema Mode is the meaningful addition: it uses TrueSpatial processing to widen video sound while holding dialogue in the center. Auto Volume, SpeechClarity calling and Immersive Audio remain part of the package. Personal Surround from the first generation is gone. These are manufacturer claims from the September 17 announcement; independent 2nd Gen tests were not available before publication.

Who it is forListeners who want the fullest premium feature set and prefer awareness to isolation.

Shokz OpenDots Air — “the value king”

The value king

A light, interchangeable clip-on with nine-hour buds, IP55 protection and most of the everyday strengths of Shokz’s $200 model.

What it does / key features

The JointArc bridge lets either 6.3g bud work on either ear. Dual 11.8mm drivers use Bassphere™ Technology and DirectPitch, while two AI-noise-canceling microphones handle calls. Tom’s Guide tested both new Shokz models for three weeks and more than 50 running miles, then called the Air “nearly as good on all fronts” while costing much less. Loud surroundings can still overwhelm any open design.

Who it is forMost buyers, especially people who wear glasses or hats and want a secure but light clip.

Baseus Bowie MC2 NC — “watch this space”

Preview, not a recommendation

Expected Oct 2026 — not on sale

Baseus is attempting the category’s hardest trick: meaningful active noise cancellation without sealing the ear canal.

What it does / key features

At IFA 2026, Baseus claimed up to -45dB ANC using dual adaptive processing and an “Air Ear Wing” intended to create a partial acoustic seal. A dual air chamber handles fit, and the case is claimed at 60 hours, presumably with ANC off. Those are extraordinary numbers for an open design — most open-ear rivals manage around -20dB of real-world reduction. And no retail unit or independent test was available before publication, so the -45dB number is still just a claim. The underlying non-NC MC2 platform earned a positive TechRadar review; that does not validate the NC model.

The $100 buy-up: Sport Open vs Ultra Open 2nd Gen

Pay more for Ultra

Choose Ultra if you listen more than you move, care about Android fidelity, or want Immersive Audio and Cinema Mode. Its buds last nine hours instead of seven.

Save with Sport

Choose Sport if you run, lift or sweat, or if aptX and spatial modes do not matter. IP54 and textured physical controls are the more practical training features.

The total battery numbers are almost equal: 29 hours for Sport versus 28.5 hours for Ultra. The premium buys features, not stamina. Short answer: runners should choose Sport; other Bose buyers should choose the flagship.

Also considered: the near-misses

  • Beyerdynamic Aventho Y: a $149.99 closed ANC over-ear headphone, sometimes miscategorized as open-ear.
  • Belkin SoundForm Isolate/Dot ($59.99/$39.99/$34.99): IFA 2026 budget sealed in-ear entries with ANC claims — not open-ear, so outside this guide's scope; too new and too thin on verification to recommend.
  • Oppo Enco X4 (China-only) / CMF Buds Neo (India-only): real 2026 launches, but sealed in-ear models — not open-ear — with no global pricing yet. CMF's actual open-ear model is the separate CMF Clip Pro.
  • Noise Master Buds Open “sound by Bose”: about €119, with late-September availability not verified.

Bottom line

Shokz OpenDots Air is the value sweet spot and the recommendation for most people. AeroClip 2 is the call-focused alternative; OpenDots 2 is the comfort-first premium pick. Bose Sport Open is the runner’s choice, while Ultra Open 2nd Gen is the premium reference once it ships. Wait for independent testing before buying the 1MORE S20 Ultra, and treat Baseus MC2 NC as a preview.

Frequently asked questions

Do open-ear earbuds sound as good as sealed designs?

Usually not for deep bass, privacy or detail in loud places. Their advantage is comfort and awareness, not isolation. A good clip-on can sound full in quiet settings, but a seal still makes low frequencies easier to reproduce.

Do any shipping open-ear models have ANC?

No model shipping in this guide has ANC. Baseus claims ANC for the Bowie MC2 NC, but it was not on sale on September 28 and had not been independently tested; most open-ear rivals manage around -20dB of real-world reduction.

Which open-ear earbuds are best for running?

Bose Sport Open is the purpose-built choice because its IP54 rating, soft cuff and physical controls suit training. Shokz OpenDots Air is the better value alternative.

Do LDAC and aptX matter on iPhone?

No. iPhones use AAC with these earbuds. LDAC and aptX Adaptive matter on compatible Android phones.

Should I wait for AeroClip 2 Pro?

The $229.99 AeroClip 2 Pro is expected in January 2027 with AI notes and eight hours of local storage. Buy the standard AeroClip 2 now if you want the current call-focused model; wait only if recording and note features are central to your use.
