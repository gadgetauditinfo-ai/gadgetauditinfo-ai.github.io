1

WUBEN E2 Pulse

The best $20 you can spend on EDC lighting — a keychain light that refuses to be boring.

Wuben built a tiny three-mode lighting tool, not just a smaller flashlight — it weighs less than a key.

The front emitter is rated at 150 lumens with a 26-metre throw, while the side COB handles close-up flood lighting. Signal modes go beyond a simple strobe: heartbeat, beacon, and red-blue warning patterns make the E2 Pulse useful for visibility as well as illumination. A magnet, quick-release keychain, USB-C charging, IP66 rating, and 10.7-gram body complete an unusually practical package. The limits matter: its battery is sealed, independent testing measured roughly 84 lumens at 30 seconds on High, and the front emitter's CRI is unspecified — reviewers note it renders color less well than the CRI 90 side light.

Who it's forAnyone who wants a light that disappears on a keyring but can still signal, stick to metal and light a work area.
Key specs150 lm claimed output; 26 m throw; side COB; heartbeat, beacon and red-blue signal modes; magnet; quick release; USB-C; IP66; 10.7 g.

Pros

  • Exceptional size-to-utility ratio
  • Useful side light and signaling modes
  • Magnetic, USB-C rechargeable, and weather resistant

Cons

  • High mode steps down quickly
  • Sealed battery cannot be replaced
  • Front emitter's CRI is unspecified (side light is rated CRI 90)
Verdict:

The best $20 you can spend on EDC lighting — just know High mode is a sprint, not a marathon.

2

Olight Oclip 2 Pro

The most capable clip light under $50 — built to point exactly where the job is.

The original Oclip was Olight's best-selling clip light, and the sequel fixes its one real weakness: the head now aims.

The light head tilts through 115 degrees while the clip rotates through 360 degrees, and self-locking pivots keep the beam where you set it. A 530-lumen flood, 125-metre spot range and 40-lumen red output cover work, distance and night-vision-friendly use. Dual magnets add mounting options, USB-C handles charging, and Olight's MCC system remains supported. The $49.99 Pro comes in Classic Black, High-Visibility Orange and Tidal Blue. A Premium version adds a 1,000 mAh charging dock, while the $69.99 aluminum Ultra — sold in Olive Green, Onyx Black and Amber Orange — exceeds this guide's budget. Olight announced it in the US on September 21, 2026.

Who it's forMechanics, campers and hands-free users who need the beam to aim rather than merely face forward.
Key specs530 lm flood; 125 m spot range; 40 lm red; 115° head tilt; 360° clip rotation; self-locking pivots; dual magnets; USB-C and MCC support.

Pros

  • Aimable head solves the original Oclip's fixed-angle limitation
  • Flood, spot and red outputs in one compact light
  • Clip and magnets support genuinely hands-free use

Cons

  • Right at the $50 ceiling
  • Premium dock and aluminum Ultra cost more
  • More pocket bulk than a keychain light
Verdict:

The most capable task light under $50 — if a tilting head and magnets matter to how you use a light, this is the one.

3

OKNIFE MK

The most tool-per-dollar in this roundup — a 52-gram three-in-one: folding blade, bit driver, and 300-lumen flashlight.

The MK's pitch is simple: the three tools you reach for most, in the one tool you'll actually carry.

A 9Cr18MoV slipjoint blade handles everyday cutting, while a 4 mm reversible S2 bit supplies PH0 Phillips and SL3.5 slotted drivers. The 300-lumen lighting system covers spotlight, flood and red modes, reaches a claimed 133 metres and can run up to 18 hours at lower output. Power comes from a rechargeable 10440 cell with USB-C charging, and a standard AAA can serve as backup. The 52-gram body carries an IP65 rating and a two-year warranty. List price is $49.99; $42.49 was reported as a launch promotion ending September 26, 2026, but that promotion was not confirmed on the official product page, so verify before buying.

Who it's forThe buyer who wants one compact answer for cutting, quick screw fixes and real illumination.
Key specs9Cr18MoV slipjoint; 4 mm PH0/SL3.5 S2 bit; spot, flood and red light; 300 lm; 133 m; up to 18 h; 10440/AAA; USB-C; 52 g; IP65.

Pros

  • Blade, driver and capable light in 52 grams
  • AAA backup adds real-world resilience
  • Spot, flood and red modes cover more than basic EDC

Cons

  • Slipjoint blade has no locking mechanism
  • Single small driver bit limits repair range
  • Reported launch discount needs verification

4

Leatherman Micra

The 30-year-old keychain legend — 10+ tools, 1.8 ounces, and the best small scissors ever made.

The Micra has been around since 1996, and Leatherman just gave it a 30th anniversary refresh without touching the formula. That restraint is the review.

The spring-action scissors remain the reason to buy it, backed by a 420HC knife, flat and Phillips screwdrivers, tweezers, nail file and cleaner, ruler and bottle opener. Closed, it measures roughly 2.5 inches and weighs about 51 grams, so it carries easily without pretending to replace a full-size multitool. Seasonal Cerakote finishes — Ginger Peach, Cardamom and North Star — refresh the familiar stainless body. Leatherman manufactures the Micra in the United States and backs it with a 25-year warranty. Current pricing sits around $49.95 to $50.

Who it's forAnyone who values excellent small scissors and proven durability more than maximum tool count.
Key specs10+ tools; spring-action scissors; 420HC knife; drivers; tweezers; nail tools; ruler; bottle opener; 2.5 in closed; 51 g; 25-year warranty.

Pros

  • Outstanding scissors for its size
  • Three decades of proven design
  • US-made with a 25-year warranty

Cons

  • Knife prevents carry through airport security
  • Costs nearly the full $50 budget
  • Drivers are for small jobs, not high torque
Verdict:

The default answer to “what's the first multi-tool I should buy” — thirty years of EDC rotations can't be wrong.

5

NITE IZE DOOHICKEY

The $8 argument for carrying a tool — a stainless keychain tool for less than a fancy coffee.

The DoohicKey is the lowest-commitment multi-tool in existence: a single piece of stainless steel that lives on your keys doing nothing until the exact moment it saves the day.

The original model packs a blunt-tip cutter, bottle opener, flat screwdriver, 1/4-, 5/16- and 3/8-inch wrenches, ruler and carabiner into one flat piece of stainless steel. There are no pivots to loosen, batteries to charge or separate bits to lose. Its blunt cutter design makes it TSA-friendly, although final screening decisions always rest with security officers. At roughly $7 to $8, the appeal is simple: a usable tool for the moment you'd otherwise have none.

Who it's forFirst-time EDC buyers, travelers and anyone who refuses pocket bulk.
Key specsDurable stainless steel body; blunt-tip cutter; bottle opener; 1/4, 5/16 and 3/8 in wrenches; ruler; screwdriver; carabiner.

Pros

  • Costs less than most replacement keyrings
  • No maintenance and almost no bulk
  • TSA-friendly design

Cons

  • Short handles limit leverage
  • Blunt cutter is less capable than a blade
  • Imperial ruler and wrench sizes are limiting outside the US
Verdict:

The cheapest way to stop saying “I wish I had a tool” — buy two, give one away.

What works—and what doesn't

What works

  • Every pick is keychain- or pocket-scale — nothing here needs a bag.
  • Three of five are launch-fresh or anniversary-fresh (MK, Oclip 2, Micra colorways), so the roundup isn't recycling last year's list.
  • The under-$50 ceiling forced real trade-off thinking, not spec bragging.

What doesn't

  • Two of five are lights — if you already own good lighting, this roundup has less for you.
  • No tracker or wallet pick fits under $50; the AirCard we like is $99.90.
  • Budget prices move fast — the MK's reported launch discount and Oclip 2's launch-week pricing need re-checking.

Bottom line

If you buy one thing from this list, make it the OKNIFE MK — nothing else under $50 packs a blade, a driver, and a real flashlight into 52 grams. If you just want to start carrying something, the $8 DoohicKey is the no-excuses first step, and the Wuben E2 Pulse is the light you'll forget you're carrying until you need it.

Frequently asked questions

Is the OKNIFE MK's launch discount still active?

It was reported to run through September 26, 2026 ($42.49 vs $49.99), but that promotion was not confirmed on the official product page; verify current pricing before buying.

Are these TSA-friendly?

Knives and multitools are generally not allowed in carry-on luggage, but checked baggage is permitted. The Nite Ize DoohicKey is labeled TSA-friendly by the manufacturer, though screening officers always make the final call.
