Minimalist phones do not sell you more features. They sell you an absence: no infinite feed, no app-store spiral, and no red badges competing for your attention. The real question is not which one is best. It is which sacrifice you can live with. These are the four options worth knowing in 2026, based on verified specifications, pricing, and availability checked on October 10.

First, the Light Phone III. This is the best all-rounder because it keeps a camera, podcasts, maps, GPS, and hotspot access without adding a browser or third-party app store. It is the minimal phone that asks you to give up the least — but batch pricing from six hundred to nine hundred dollars makes that convenience expensive. Check your carrier and the current price before ordering.

Second, the Mudita Kompakt. It is the easiest starting point: it is shipping now, its hardware Offline Plus switch cuts the microphone and cellular modem, and sideloading gives you one escape hatch for an essential app. The trade-off is paying four hundred dollars for a slow E Ink screen and a basic document camera.

Third, the Punkt MP02. This is the purist's choice: calls, texts, encrypted Pigeon messaging, and a 4G hotspot. Nothing else. The design and call quality are the appeal; limited stock and uneven carrier support are the warning. It makes the most sense on AT&T.

Finally, the Minimal Phone 2. Its physical QWERTY keyboard is the most interesting idea here for writers and note-takers. But it is still a Kickstarter campaign, not a finished retail product. Back the six-hundred-dollar tier only if you accept the December estimate and normal crowdfunding risk. Do not choose it if you need a phone now.
