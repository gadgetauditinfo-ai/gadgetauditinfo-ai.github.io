Your sleep data is the most personal data you own. It knows when you're unconscious, where you live, and whether your heart is misbehaving. So this guide starts with the question most sleep-tech roundups bury: what does it cost you, and what does it cost every month? These are the seven sleep gadgets actually worth buying in 2026 — every one buyable right now at a verified price, every one with its subscription situation stated up front. Two rules ran the whole selection: if it needs a subscription to do the thing it was sold for, you get told the three-year cost. And nothing here is crowdfunded, pre-order, or “launching soon” — a sleep gadget that doesn't exist yet can't fix your Tuesday night.

1. Hatch Sleep Clock — the one that watches your sleep without touching you

What it is

The contactless sleep tracker that became a real product in 2026 — a bedside device that monitors breathing and movement with radar. No camera, no microphone pointed at you, no wearable.

What it does

Tracks sleep stages, breathing, and movement from your nightstand (97% sleep-detection accuracy vs clinical polysomnography per Hatch's Somnofy validation), then builds a nightly plan and a sunrise alarm around what it measured. The data loop is the pitch: it measures, it plans, it wakes you in the light phase.

Who it’s forThe person who wants sleep data but won't wear a ring or a watch to bed, and won't put a camera or a listening mic on the nightstand. The no-camera/no-mic/no-wearable triple is the whole selling point.

Key specs (verified)

Sleep detection97% sleep-detection accuracy vs clinical polysomnography (Somnofy validation, per Hatch PR)
SensorsNo camera, no mic, no wearable — radar-based
Wake-upSunrise alarm
Connectivity2.4GHz WiFi + Bluetooth setup
Price$249.99

Price & subscription

$249.99 at hatch.co (buyable now). The first-ever discount — $199.99 during Prime Big Deal Days, Oct 6–7 — is over; that's the price to watch if it comes back for Black Friday. Sleep tracking, insights, and the nightly plan need NO subscription. Hatch+ ($5.99/mo or $59.99/yr per Hatch's launch PR, with a 30-day trial) is optional and only buys the 2,500+ sound library. Yawnder has flagged conflicting Hatch+ prices on Hatch's own pages — verify at checkout.

Pros

  • Contactless tracking with no camera, no mic, no wearable — the privacy story no ring or watch can tell
  • 97% detection accuracy vs polysomnography is an unusually strong validation claim for a consumer device
  • The core sleep-tracking loop needs no subscription — the paid tier is optional — it sells sounds, not data
  • Sunrise alarm + nightly plan built from your own measured sleep, not generic tips

Cons

  • $249.99 is bedside-device money for a single-purpose box — a smartwatch does this plus everything else (with a screen on your wrist all night)
  • Hatch+ pricing is inconsistent on Hatch's own site — a small trust crack on the thing they're most transparent about
  • 2.4GHz-only WiFi in 2026 will annoy mesh-network households
  • One sleeper only — partners need their own clock and their own $250
Verdict

The best buyable contactless sleep tracker, and the only pick that never touches you. Buy it if a wearable to bed is a non-starter; skip it if you already wear a watch at night and it's doing this job.

2. Soundcore Sleep A30 — the ANC sleep earbud that became the reference

What it is

The mainstream ANC sleep earbud — from the brand that made sleep earbuds a normal product instead of a Kickstarter curiosity.

What it does

Smart ANC up to 30 dB plus adaptive snore masking (the case listens to the room and the buds mask it), sleep monitoring with position tracking in the Soundcore app, all in a 3g-per-bud package.

Who it’s forLight sleepers whose enemy is noise — snoring partners, thin walls, city hum. If you already know you're a sound-first sleeper, this is the mainstream answer.

Key specs (verified)

Noise controlSmart ANC up to 30 dB + adaptive snore masking
Battery~9h local / 45h total
Fit3g per bud, ~7% slimmer than the A20
TrackingSleep monitoring with position tracking
WirelessBluetooth 5.4

Price & subscription

$199.99 list at soundcore.com, in stock — no subscription (Soundcore's own FSA listing confirms the Calm content needs none). It hit $159.99 on Amazon during Prime Big Deal Days; we couldn't verify a live discount today, so $199.99 is the real number.

Pros

  • The snore-masking loop is the real feature: the case hears the room and the buds mask it, not just mute it
  • 3g per bud and 7% slimmer than the A20 — this generation actually chased the fit problem
  • No subscription — the whole sleep stack works at purchase price
  • 9h local battery covers a full night with margin; the case holds 45h total

Cons

  • The Soundcore Sleep Earbuds 4 ($229.99) and 4 Pro ($349.99) are announced for October/November 2026 with PPG heart tracking and a touchscreen case — buying the A30 today is buying one generation before the flagship turn. The real question: do you need a year of silence now, or wait for October?
  • $199.99 is real money for earbuds that do exactly one thing well (sleep) and little else
  • Snore masking works on steady noise; it won't save you from a partner with arrhythmic, freight-train snoring
Verdict

The mainstream sleep-earbud pick and the safest recommendation in the category. Buy the A30 now if noise is your problem; if you want heart-rate data too, wait for the 4 Pro in November.

3. Ozlo Sleepbuds 2 — the premium passive pick, from the Bose Sleepbuds heirs

What it is

The second generation of the sleep bud built by the team that inherited the old Bose Sleepbuds' soul — passive isolation and sound masking instead of ANC.

What it does

Combines passive isolation with adaptive sound masking, backs it with environmental sensing (temperature, light, noise, humidity), and runs 14 hours on a single charge — enough for multiple nights of streaming between charges.

Who it’s forThe sleeper who finds ANC buds uncomfortable for eight hours and wants masking sound — rain, brown noise — instead of silence. And side sleepers: passive buds beat ANC buds on pressure.

Key specs (verified)

Battery14h single-charge streaming; case holds 2–3 nights
Noise controlPassive isolation + adaptive sound masking
SensorsTemperature, light, noise, humidity
ControlsPhone-free snooze/play button on the case
Fit4 tip sizes
Price$279

Price & subscription

$279 at ozlosleep.com, buyable now, no subscription.

Pros

  • 14h single-charge battery is the best in the category — charge every few nights, not every morning
  • The environmental sensors (room temp, light, noise, humidity) explain WHY a night was bad, not just that it was
  • The case button lets you snooze or play masking sound without touching your phone at 3 a.m.
  • No subscription, and the passive-isolation design is kinder to side sleepers than ANC in the ear

Cons

  • $279 is the priciest earbud-style pick in this guide — $80 over the A30 for a different philosophy, not a strictly better one
  • No ANC: masking sound needs to be running to do its job, so it's not for people who sleep in true silence
  • No PPG or heart tracking — it's a sleep-audio device with sensors for the room, not a biometric tracker
Verdict

The premium pick for masking-sound sleepers and side sleepers. If ANC buds feel like pressure in your ears all night, this is your lane — otherwise the A30 costs $80 less.

A screenless smart band from a wedding-ring brand that walked into sleep tracking with one weapon: no subscription.

Tracks sleep stages and scores, heart rate, HRV, SpO2, stress, skin temperature, and women's health, distills it into a proprietary 1–100 Q Score, and runs 12+ days per charge. No screen, no monthly bill.

$199.95 at qalo.com, on sale now. NO subscription — that's the headline selling point. FSA/HSA eligible.

5. Somnee Smart Sleep Headband — the neurostimulation outlier (with the membership tax)

What it is

The only pick that tries to CHANGE your sleep instead of measuring it — a headband from a company founded by Berkeley sleep scientist Dr. Matthew Walker, using personalized neurostimulation before bed.

What it does

A 15-minute pre-bed session of personalized tACS neurostimulation with clinical-grade EEG monitoring, delivered through bone-conduction speakers. You don't wear it overnight — it's a pre-sleep ritual, not a monitor.

Who it’s forThe buyer who's tried the trackers and the earbuds and still can't fall asleep — the onset-insomnia lane. This is an intervention, not a diary.

Key specs (verified)

Session15-min personalized neurostimulation (tACS) + clinical-grade EEG
AudioBone-conduction speakers (gen 2)
ControlsPhone-free button start
Company claims~50% faster sleep onset, ~33% fewer wake-ups

Price & subscription

"From $389 (plus membership)" at somneesleep.com; the Smart Sleep Bundle is $489 including 6 months of membership. Membership is REQUIRED for daily personalization — and the ongoing rate is unverified, which is itself a flag. HSA/FSA eligible.

Pros

  • The only product here that intervenes instead of observing — actually different, not a better dashboard
  • Founded by Dr. Matthew Walker (UC Berkeley) — real sleep-science credibility, not a wellness brand with a rebrand
  • You don't wear it overnight — for people who hate sleeping in gear, the 15-minute session is the whole product
  • Clinical-grade EEG means the personalization is measured, not vibes

Cons

  • Membership is mandatory and the ongoing rate is unverified — buying hardware that bricks into a dumb headband if you stop paying is the worst version of the subscription model
  • $489 with 6 months included is the most expensive entry in this guide, before the recurring cost
  • The efficacy claims (~50% faster onset) are company claims — the clinical literature on consumer tACS for insomnia is still thin
  • Bone-conduction audio and a headband for 15 minutes every night is a ritual — rituals fail where habits succeed

Official product video is unavailable

No official Somnee product video exists anywhere — Somnee has published no brand or campaign film; the entry stays video-free rather than showing a reviewer copy.

Verdict

The most interesting device in the guide and the hardest to recommend. Buy it if onset insomnia is your actual problem and you've exhausted the cheap fixes; don't buy it until Somnee publishes the ongoing membership rate plainly.

Medical channel

The medical-channel alternative: Happy Ring

Happy Ring — $396 via Happy Health. FDA-cleared (Class II) for at-home sleep-apnea diagnosis: the ring's AI analyzes your data and a physician in Happy Health's network can diagnose and prescribe CPAP or dental devices, with a claimed ~97% accuracy vs lab studies. The $396 includes one year of sleep tracking plus physician consultations, and it's often covered under insurance. Post-year-1 pricing is unverified — ask before you buy. This isn't a retail product, it's a medical channel, which is why it sits outside the ranked picks. But if your suspicion is sleep APNEA rather than bad habits, a diagnosis is worth more than any tracker in this guide.

Frequently asked questions

Do any of these need a subscription?

Two of the seven. The Oura Ring 5 locks full features behind $5.99/mo ($69.99/yr). Somnee requires a membership for daily personalization — and its ongoing rate isn't published, which we flag as a con. Everything else — Hatch tracking, Soundcore, Ozlo, QALO, SleepPhones — works at purchase price. Hatch+ ($5.99/mo) is optional and buys only a sound library.

Can a gadget diagnose sleep apnea?

Only one: Happy Ring is FDA-cleared (Class II) for at-home sleep-apnea diagnosis with a physician in the loop. Everything else in this guide tracks and estimates — none of it is a medical diagnosis. If you stop breathing at night, see a doctor, not a roundup.

Why not just use my smartwatch?

You can — and a watch you already own is the cheapest sleep tracker. What it can't do: sleep comfortably for everyone (watches are bulky in bed), contactless tracking (Hatch), snore masking (A30), or neurostimulation (Somnee). The picks here earn their place on things a watch doesn't do.

Which pick for a snoring partner?

Soundcore Sleep A30 — the adaptive snore masking is the category's only answer that reacts to the room. The Ozlo's masking sound is the passive alternative. Foam earplugs are the $5 answer.

Why isn't the Tom's Guide pick here?

Tom's Guide's sleep-tracker guide is 278 days stale (their “Best sleep trackers 2026” still reviews the Oura Ring 4) — which is exactly why this guide exists: the sleep-tech wave of 2026 moved, and the mainstream roundups haven't.
