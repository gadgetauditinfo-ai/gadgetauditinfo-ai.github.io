LAUNCH 01 · LOGITECH

Logitech Blue Yeti 2 — “the Yeti, finally rebooted”

Buy · On sale now

The first true sequel to the 2009 podcasting workhorse — with a 3D voice-proximity sensor that adjusts gain, EQ, and pickup pattern as you move.

What it does / key features

The Yeti defined USB podcasting in 2009 and then sat essentially unchanged while an entire creator economy grew up around it. The Yeti 2 — launched at Logitech G PLAY on September 24, the first major update since Logitech bought Blue in 2018 — is the reboot. The headline feature is a 3D voice-proximity sensor that tracks how close you are to the mic and automatically adjusts gain, EQ, and pickup pattern. That's the thing beginners get wrong most: inconsistent mic distance. Around it: an on-device AI de-noiser for keyboard and fan noise, a 1.3-inch color LED display with a reactive Yeti mascot (customizable via G Hub), USB-C at 24-bit/48kHz, a redesigned 3-capsule array, Blue VO!CE DSP, and pattern steering across Stereo, Cardioid, Super/Hyper Cardioid, Omni, and Bidirectional. Five on-device presets, capacitive mute, 3.5mm monitoring.

Who it's forNew and growing podcasters, streamers, and voiceover work — anyone whose mic technique is currently “sit here and hope.” The obvious upgrade path for everyone whose channel outgrew a headset mic.
AvailabilityOn sale now from Logitech G — $159.99 US (black & white); Australia shows pre-order, ships Oct 13.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Seventeen years of “the Yeti but better” demand, answered by the actual brand — the ecosystem of mounts, arms, and know-how already exists.
  • The proximity auto-adjust fixes the number-one beginner failure mode: drifting off-mic mid-sentence.
  • At $159.99 it sits well under the Shure MV7's $299 — the podcaster staple — with a sensor trick the MV7 doesn't have.
  • Early reactions are positive on the auto-adjustments, which is the feature that actually matters here.

  • USB-C only; no XLR on the spec sheet, so there's no path to a pro mixer later without replacing the mic.

LAUNCH 02 · RAY-BAN META

Ray-Ban Meta Gen 3 — “the camera glasses, grown up”

Buy · On sale now

Meta's third-gen smart glasses — 12MP camera, 3K video, six-mic array — on sale now at $449.

What it does / key features

The Gen 3 is Meta's refinement play, announced at Meta Connect on September 23 with hands-ons the same day from Engadget, WIRED, and Tom's Guide. The camera steps to 12MP with 3K Ultra HD video, the mic array goes to six (Meta claims it cuts over 90% of background noise), battery stretches to 9 hours — an hour more than Gen 2 — and there's a customizable action button for Meta AI, with Meta's Muse agent coming to all glasses models. Twenty-seven color and lens combos across three styles — Wayfarer, Aviator, and the new cat-eye Zena — plus a limited-edition vintage Aviator for Ray-Ban's 90th anniversary, prescription support (expected, but Meta hasn't confirmed lens options at launch), and a built-in capture LED. Meta also says these will later be the first consumer devices capturing and playing back video in Dolby Atmos with 360° spatial sound. For creators, the real story is hands-free POV capture: b-roll without holding a phone.

Who it's forCreators who want first-person capture, and anyone already in Meta's AI ecosystem who never flinched at the camera.

LAUNCH 04 · UGREEN

UGREEN MagFlow Pro — “the power bank with a radiator”

Buy · Retail — check live price

A liquid-cooled Qi2.2 25W magnetic power bank — 10,000mAh with a visible coolant loop and a status display. $149.99, but check the live price.

What it does / key features

Wireless charging's dirty secret is heat: 25W magnetic charging cooks both the bank and the phone, and everything throttles. UGREEN's answer — which it calls the world's first liquid-cooled Qi2 25W magnetic bank — is a CryoPulse liquid micro-pump with a transparent coolant window, so you can watch the cooling work, plus a small LCD showing battery, cycle count, cooling status, and power output. Underneath: 10,000mAh, 25W wireless, Up to 45W wired output (built-in USB-C cable plus an extra USB-C port — UGREEN's spec; two independent reviews have reported the built-in cable at 30W), three devices at once. The independent review wave from late September is unusually aligned: 9to5Toys tested it and called it “a completely different league” at keeping temperatures in check — the bank stayed cool while wireless-charging an iPhone 16 Pro, with a strong magnet noted. Apple World Today gave it 4/5: powerful but bulky — “built like a brick,” squared edges, an awkward pre-installed cable loop during wireless charging. GeekExtreme's take is the useful one: the cooling works on the bank itself more than the phone, and at $149.99 it's hard to justify — but UGREEN's MagFlow fall deals run September 28 to October 11 (up to 50% off), and it's been seen at $100–$99.98. At that price the math gets much friendlier. Available now from UGREEN's official store and Amazon.

Who it's foriPhone MagSafe users who actually run 25W wireless charging long enough to feel the heat — and desk-bound charger nerds who like their gear to look like engineering.
AvailabilityRetail now from UGREEN's official store and Amazon — MSRP $149.99; fall deals Sept 28–Oct 11 seen at $99.98–$100. Check the live price.

LAUNCH 05 · ANKER

Anker Nano Charger Neo — “the $26 charger that embarrasses Apple's”

Buy · On sale now

A $25.99 single-port USB-C charger with 45W sustained and 65W dynamic output — smaller than Apple's $34.99 adapter, and faster.

What it does / key features

The 2026 charger differentiator is AVS — Adjustable Voltage Supply, finer voltage steps that let a charger negotiate smarter with the phone — and Anker just took it mainstream at $25.99. The Nano Charger Neo does 45W sustained with up to 65W dynamic via AVS, ActiveShield 4.0 temperature monitoring, and a 180° foldable plug, on sale now through Anker's official storefront. Anker claims an iPhone 18 Pro Max to 80% in about 32 minutes — that's the company's figure, not an independent test — and Notebookcheck notes it undercuts Apple's 40W Dynamic Power Adapter ($34.99) while being smaller. One port, one job, done right.

Who it's forAnyone who charges one device at a time and is tired of paying the Apple tax for a wall wart.

LAUNCH 06 · LETPOT

LetPot LPH-Home — “the indoor garden that measures itself”

Back · Live Kickstarter campaign

A smart two-tier indoor garden — up to 32 plants, each tier with its own 6L reservoir and independent EC monitoring. Super Early Bird $398.99 on Kickstarter.

What it does / key features

LetPot (founded 2019) is an established smart-garden brand, and the LPH-Home is its crowdfunding play: two tiers, each with its own 6L water tank and separate EC (nutrient) monitoring, so you can grow leafy greens below and taller fruiting plants above on different feed schedules. Thirty-two planting pods, 36W + 24W full-spectrum LEDs, a 12–22-inch adjustable upper height for tomatoes and peppers as they mature, bamboo-forward design, a TFT touchscreen, and a free LetPot app — with no subscription, which in 2026 is a feature worth naming. The system is open: your own seeds and compatible sponges, not a seed-pod subscription. Water-level and EC alerts, removable casters, reservoirs that separate from the electronics for rinsing. The Kickstarter launched October 1 at 11:00 AM ET — LetPot's own site confirms it's live now — with a public Super Early Bird of $398.99; a $10 VIP reservation got $50 in store vouchers plus priority delivery, and early VIPs also received two 172-piece seed pod kits. The honest pitch: indoor gardens fail at nutrient management, and dual-zone EC monitoring is the feature that addresses the actual failure mode.

Who it's forApartment dwellers who want real harvests — herbs, greens, fruiting plants — not a decorative succulent. Smart-home people who like their gadgets to do chores.

Frequently asked questions

Is this hands-on tested?

No. This is a research review: every spec was verified from manufacturer pages, live campaign confirmations, and reputable press on October 2, 2026. Verdicts are research verdicts — we never claim to have used a product we haven't.

What's the difference between buy, pre-order, and back?

Buy means a retail product shipping now. Pre-order means money committed before shipping (here, from Meta — an established company). Back means a Kickstarter pledge: your money is committed before the product exists, and ship dates are hopes, not promises.
