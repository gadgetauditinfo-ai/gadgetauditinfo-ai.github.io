LetPot LPH-Home — the two-tier smart garden that actually watches your nutrients

Back — verified live with real traction.

Most smart gardens are dumb planters with a light on top. The LPH-Home's trick is genuinely rare at this price: each of its two growing tiers gets its own 6-liter reservoir and its own EC (nutrient) monitoring, so you can run herbs up top and fruiting plants below without compromise.

What it does / key features

Two independent growing zones, 32 plants max, 36W upper + 24W lower full-spectrum LEDs, 12–22 inch adjustable upper height, TFT touchscreen, free app with no subscription, vacation mode, bamboo-forward frame.

Who it's for: Indoor gardeners who've outgrown single-zone towers and want to stop guessing about nutrients.

Image: leagend · official brand

leagend JS300 PRO — the jump starter you install once and forget about

Interesting — but wait for tier pricing.

Every portable jump starter shares one failure mode: its own battery dies in the trunk exactly when you need it. leagend's answer is to delete the battery entirely — a supercapacitor unit you permanently install, which draws from the car's own electrical system and is always ready.

What it does / key features

Battery-free supercapacitor starting assistance, permanent vehicle installation, and a smart app showing SoC, SoH, CCA, voltage, starter voltage, temperatures, starting performance and history. Self-Learning Mode and Drive Mode are included; PRO-D adds a solar-assisted onboard display so you can check battery health without opening the app.

Who it's for: Drivers in extreme climates, fleet operators, and anyone who's been burned by a dead jump-starter battery.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Permanent install kills the dead-battery failure mode completely.
  • SoH/CCA monitoring makes it a diagnostic tool, not just an emergency device.
  • −40°C to 80°C range is the real supercapacitor advantage over lithium.
  • 20-year brand with existing supercap products.

Cons

  • Campaign pricing completely undisclosed — you cannot judge value yet.
  • Permanent install is a commitment: fit, wiring, and resale-transfer hassle versus a portable unit.
  • “Self-Learning Mode” and “Drive Mode” are marketing terms with no technical detail published.
  • No peak-amp figure published for the PRO line.

FansDreams Folio — a real desk that folds down like a book

Back — strong creator, aggressive early-bird.

Laptop stands are portable but useless as tables; folding tables are useful but bulky. The Folio's pitch is the middle that actually works: a real tabletop that folds to an A5 book, 30 mm thick, and unfolds in seconds into four configurations.

What it does / key features

Four modes — mouse pad, laptop stand, lap desk, and side table — with no assembly. The hinge carries an 8 kg company load rating and completed a 100,000-cycle internal fold test. Standard opens to 440×240×210 mm and folds to 220×240×30 mm; Max opens to 540×245×255 mm and folds to 270×245×30 mm.

Who it's for: Remote workers, students, and travelers who work from beds, sofas, cars, and cafes.

Frequently asked questions

When will ComboX and the HOZO Hobby Knife actually go live?

Both were announced for October 13, but their campaign pages were still pre-launch at our 07:55 PKT check. Launches often flip during US daytime hours; we'll update this page when they do.

Are the early-bird prices real discounts?

Where both prices are published (Folio, REZE FLOW, LetPot's SEB), yes — but always compare against the stated retail/MSRP on the live page, not the PR.

What if a campaign fails to deliver?

That's the crowdfunding risk: you're pre-ordering, not buying. Prefer established creators with shipped products, and never pledge money you can't afford to wait on.

Why are there no buy links?

Real affiliate links get wired when the owner supplies them; we don't invent URLs.

Is the pledged total a quality signal?

It's a traction signal, not a quality signal. A fast-funding campaign from a first-time creator is still a first-time creator.
