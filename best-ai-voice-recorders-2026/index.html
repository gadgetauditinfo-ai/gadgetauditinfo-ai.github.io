BEST OVERALL

PLAUD Note

The safest buy in the category — mature hardware, native call recording, and software that has had time to grow up.

If your week moves between phone calls, room meetings, and video calls, the original PLAUD Note still has the cleanest answer.

The signature is dual-mode recording: it captures phone calls as well as in-person conversations. The 0.12-inch, 1.06-ounce card attaches magnetically to a phone, with a case and ring included. It records for up to 30 hours, waits up to 60 days on standby, and stores audio on 64GB locally.

The software is the reason this remains our top pick. PLAUD handles transcription in 112 languages with speaker labels, mind maps, more than 10,000 summary templates, and Ask Plaud across its app, web, and desktop clients. The desktop app also records Zoom, Meet, and Teams calls. You get 300 transcription minutes per month at no extra cost; beyond that, the subscription becomes part of the purchase.

BEST WEARABLE

PLAUD NotePin S

Buy it for the form factor — if a card recorder sounds awkward to carry, this is the answer.

The NotePin S is the round-up's most forgettable recorder in the best possible sense: wear it as a necklace, wristband, clip, or magnetic pin and get on with the conversation.

At 17.4g, it is the lightest polished wearable here, and the full accessory kit ships in the box. The S model's meaningful upgrade is a physical button: long-press to start recording, short-press to place a highlight marker. That is a practical correction to the original NotePin's fiddly haptic control.

Inside are two MEMS microphones, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth 5.2, 64GB for roughly 480 hours of audio, up to 20 hours of continuous recording, and a 40-day standby claim. It shares PLAUD's 112-language transcription, speaker labels, 10,000-plus templates, and Ask Plaud. The included 300 monthly minutes require no paid subscription; Pro is about $99.99 per year for 1,200 minutes (billed annually), while Unlimited is $19.99 per month (billed annually; month-to-month costs more).

BEST TWO-IN-ONE

viaim RecDot

The pick for people who would rather not carry a second device.

RecDot is the only recorder here that can replace something already in your pocket: it is a daily pair of true wireless earbuds and an AI meeting recorder in one.

The earbud hardware is credible on its own: 11mm titanium-coated drivers, Bluetooth 5.2, LHDC and Hi-Res certification, 48dB hybrid ANC, and three microphones plus a bone-conduction mic in each earbud. FlashRecord starts a capture from the case or the buds without opening the app. Battery life is up to nine hours per earbud and 36 hours with the case; charging is USB-C or Qi, and the buds carry an IPX5 rating.

The app turns recordings into real-time transcripts, summaries, to-dos, and mind maps across 78 languages, or 145 when dialects are counted. Its 600 free transcription minutes per month are the most generous allowance in this guide. Digital Trends called it a great choice for the right buyer, while Gadget Review called it the best version of a new category.

BEST TASK ASSISTANT

SwitchBot AI MindClip

The smartest software in the roundup, priced accordingly — buy it if you want an assistant, not just a recorder.

MindClip tries to remember what happened next, not merely what was said: tasks, deadlines, promises, reminders, and the context around them.

The 16.8g clip attaches to a collar, shirt, or bag strap. It carries 64GB of local storage, records for more than 20 hours, hears within a claimed three-meter near-field range, and waits more than 40 days on standby. A slide switch starts capture and a blinking LED makes recording visible. Speaker recognition, separation, and voiceprint ID sort multi-person conversations.

Qwen-Audio-ASR handles transcription, while a RAG-powered Ask AI layer searches conversational memory. Transcripts become summaries, key points, to-dos, and reminders organized into Timeline, Priority, Daily Memory, and Weekly Summary views. Google and Apple Calendar and Reminders sync are built in, and an OpenAPI CLI hook can feed workflow tools. Pairing and transfers are encrypted; SwitchBot cites EN18031 plus ISO 27001 and 27701 certification.

BEST OPEN-SOURCE PICK

Omi

The wildcard — if per-minute billing is the dealbreaker, start here.

Omi is the anti-subscription option: an open-source pendant built around ambient memory rather than a tidy stack of meeting files.

Wear it as a necklace or pendant, clip it on, or use a medical-tape mount. It is designed for 10 to 14 hours of daily wear and records online or offline before syncing later. The platform supports more than 25 languages and turns conversations into summaries, to-dos, meeting scheduling, and questions over past conversations.

The open hardware and software support community-built plugins through its app marketplace. Omi advertises “No subscription required” and a “Free plan included, forever.” That is the cleanest pricing philosophy here, but there is an important caveat: $129 at publication (limited-time sale; $179 list) — verify at omi.me before buying. Ambient always-on capture also makes consent a front-and-center decision rather than a footnote.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a subscription to use an AI voice recorder?

Not to start. PLAUD Note, NotePin S, and MindClip include 300 transcription minutes per month, while RecDot includes 600. Omi advertises no subscription requirement and a free plan. The recorder can still capture audio when a paid allowance is exhausted, but AI transcription and summary features are the part most likely to be limited.

Which recorder is best for meetings?

PLAUD Note is the safest all-round meeting pick because it covers phone calls, in-person rooms, and desktop video meetings through one software stack. Choose NotePin S instead when hands-free wear matters more than call capture.

Which is best for phone calls?

PLAUD Note. It is the only pick in this guide with native phone-call recording as a headline feature, and it switches between call and in-person recording modes.

Is it legal to record conversations with these?

Recording law depends on where you and the other participants are. Some places allow one-party consent; others require everyone to agree. Treat the recorder's LED or wearable design as a reminder, not permission: tell people, obtain the consent required locally, and do not record sensitive spaces casually.

Can my phone do this instead — and why would I pay for a dedicated device?

Yes. A phone can record and a transcription app can process the file. Dedicated hardware earns its place through faster physical controls, better wearability, phone-call capture in PLAUD Note's case, and less friction during long sessions. Occasional recorders should try the phone workflow first; a dedicated device makes sense only when that friction repeats every week.
