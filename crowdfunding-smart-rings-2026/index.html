Almost every serious challenger to Oura started as a crowdfunding campaign: RingConn raised roughly $4 million on Kickstarter before becoming the default recommendation; Ultrahuman started on Kickstarter and Indiegogo before hitting Best Buy and Costco. Rings are the one wearable where the incumbent’s subscription is the real competitor — and crowdfunding is where the subscription-free rebels still launch.

Two picks you can buy retail today with zero subscription. Two live Kickstarter campaigns — one we think is worth the gamble, one we’re flagging with a real warning. And two to watch: one that only makes sense alongside Even’s glasses, and one with no price or date yet.

RingConn Gen 3 — “the ring that buzzes”

Buy · Retail and shipping

A subscription-free titanium health ring with a vibration motor — the first mainstream ring that can tap you on the finger, not just report on you.

What it does / key features

RingConn Gen 3 is the boring-in-the-best-way pick: a full health ring (heart rate, HRV, skin temperature, sleep staging with AI coaching, sleep-apnea screening carried over from Gen 2) that charges no subscription, ever. The thing that makes it different from every other health ring here is the haptic motor. It can vibrate for an elevated heart rate, a step goal hit, or a low battery — turning a passive tracker into something that actually interrupts you. It also tracks vascular health trends (sleep-time blood-pressure patterns — NOT a clinical measurement, which RingConn states outright). Battery is the best claimed in the category: 11–14 days with vibration off, 10–12 with it on, in a 2.3mm, 2.5–3.5g titanium body. Sizes 6–15, IP68/10ATM, wireless charging case, iOS 17+ and Android 10+.

Who it's forAnyone who wants Oura-style tracking without the $5.99/month tax, and anyone who finds the passive "yesterday's report" nature of health rings useless — the vibration gives it daytime value no other health ring here has.
PositionTop pick · Subscription-free health tracking

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Zero subscription — at $349 flat it undercuts Oura's real price ($349 + $5.99/mo) by hundreds over three years.
  • The best claimed battery here: up to 14 days against Galaxy Ring's 7.
  • The vibration motor is a genuine category first — health rings that tap you back are more useful than ones that just track.
  • A proven brand: RingConn's Gen 1 raised ~$1.245M from 7,000+ Indiegogo backers and Gen 2 raised ~$4M from ~16,000 Kickstarter backers; 800,000+ units shipped in 190 countries.

Cons

  • $349–369 is premium pricing — you're paying for the subscription you never owe.
  • Vascular tracking is patterns-only; don't buy it expecting medical-grade blood-pressure readings.
  • The vibration is deliberately limited — no call or text notifications, so it's not a notification device.
Verdict

The best Oura alternative in this roundup and the top pick for health-ring buyers. Retail, shipping, no subscription — it earns the crown.

Ultrahuman Ring Air — “the graduate”

Buy · Proven retail product

The proven Oura rival — a titanium metabolism and sleep ring that started on Kickstarter and grew into Best Buy shelves.

What it does / key features

The Ring Air is what a crowdfunded gadget looks like after it works: Kickstarter's own blog ran the "from Kickstarter to a store near you" story on it. Three years shipping, a $140M revenue run rate, a $70M round that included backing from Qualcomm Ventures, and retail availability at Amazon, Best Buy, and Costco with a free sizing kit. The tracking suite is the richest subscription-free one here: sleep (its strongest suit, per reviewers), HRV, skin temperature, ovulation prediction, caffeine windows, and an AI layer called JADE, plus an expanding PowerPlugs store. Core data carries no mandatory subscription — though that's getting blurry (PowerPlugs like Respiratory Health monetize at $4/month or $40/year, which is the asterisk). Build is titanium with tungsten carbide carbon, sizes 5–14.

Who it's forHealth-ring buyers who want the most features per dollar with no monthly bill for the core data — and who'd rather buy retail with a sizing kit than back a campaign.
PositionSubscription-free maximalist pick

Verified specifications

Charging3-hour full charge
Sizes5–14, free sizing kit

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Software updates arrive faster than the Oura's — Digital Trends noted the Air received more updates in a single month than the Oura Ring had most of that year.
  • The most features without a monthly bill — sleep, HRV, temp, ovulation, caffeine windows, AI insights.
  • Real retail availability with a sizing kit — no guessing your size from a printable chart.
  • The category's best crowdfunding-graduation story: ~$400k Kickstarter 2022 + Indiegogo, launched June 2023, now in Costco.

Cons

  • Battery claims are optimistic: expect 3–4 days, not 6 — and the 3-hour charge is the slowest here.
  • "No subscription ever" is blurring as PowerPlugs monetize more features.
  • Verify US availability before buying: a 2025 patent dispute with Oura reportedly led to an import ban, with Ultrahuman's stated response being a US factory (Texas) — that is the company's own plan; one outlet reported an ITC ruling that the company had photoshopped its logos onto others' factory photos — the situation is fluid.
Verdict

The subscription-free maximalist pick. If you want the most data and never want a monthly bill for the basics, this is the graduate that proved the crowdfunding model.

KiWear Ring — “the remote control for everything”

Cautious back · Live Kickstarter campaign

A ceramic gesture ring that turns finger flicks into commands across phones, TVs, laptops, and smart glasses — health tracking is the side dish.

What it does / key features

KiWear flips the smart-ring formula: this is an input device, not a tracker. An IMU motion sensor in a ~5g ceramic band reads thumb swipes (scroll), taps (click), air circles (volume), and check-marks (answer calls), acting as an air mouse and remote across phones, tablets, computers, TVs, projectors, smart glasses, and car screens over Bluetooth. It can hold multiple paired devices and knock-to-switch between up to 9 of them. Hands-on reviewers (Cas and Chary XR, whose hands-on was sponsored content paid for by KiWear) say the gestures feel like Meta's Neural Band despite IMU-only sensing. It still tracks heart rate, sleep, activity, and SpO2 trends in the background. No screen, no LED on the ring itself; 5ATM water resistance; magnetic charging.

Who it's forScreen jugglers — presenters, smart-glasses early adopters, anyone who'd trade a drawer of remotes for one ring. XR users get the most value (the DeX + glasses demos are the best use case we've seen).
Campaign status$249 Super Early Bird. Shipping is expected in late November 2026, but every campaign date is a hope, not a promise.

Even Realities R1 — “the glasses' sidekick”

Buy only for Even G2

The companion control ring for the Even G2 display smart glasses — tap, swipe, and press your way through the in-lens display.

What it does / key features

The R1 exists for one job: discreet input for the Even G2 glasses (the $599 display glasses with no camera — the least-creepy smart-glasses ecosystem going). Instead of pawing at your temple or talking to the air in public, you tap, swipe, and press on the ring; TriSync (ring–glasses–phone) keeps the gestures synced. The build is jewelry-grade: zirconia ceramic plus stainless steel. It also tracks heart rate, SpO2, sleep quality, skin temperature, and activity, with a "Productivity Score" wellness metric in the app. It's actually shipping, it's actually reviewed — which counts for a lot in a category full of promises.

Who it's forEven G2 glasses owners (or people about to become them) who want the private, no-camera smart-glasses experience without the public weirdness of voice or touchpad input.
PositionEven G2 companion only

Verified specifications

ControlGesture control of G2 display via TriSync
HealthHR, SpO2, sleep quality, skin temp, activity
Price$249 standalone (the 50%-off launch bundle at $124.50 with the $599 G2 glasses has expired)

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Shipping now and already reviewed — no campaign risk, no pre-order patience.
  • Discreet input beats shouting at glasses or smudging the temple arm in public.
  • The jewelry-grade build is the nicest hardware here.

Cons

  • $249 on top of $599 glasses is a lot for an accessory; standalone it's pointless outside Even's ecosystem.
  • Learning curve plus accidental activations (ZDNET) — you'll trigger things you didn't mean to at first.
  • Health tracking is basic next to dedicated rings, and launch software was "still being polished."
Verdict

Buy it only as the G2's companion. If you're in Even's ecosystem, it's the right remote; if you're not, skip it entirely.

Spark Ring — “the risky one”

Watch, don't back · Privacy claim unresolved

A $159 Kickstarter AI voice ring that records your fleeting ideas with a double-tap — with unresolved questions big enough that we're not recommending it yet.

What it does / key features

Spark Ring (from SparkRing Inc — founder Charley Tang, formerly of ByteDance's LLM benchmarks group) is the cheapest AI ring we've seen: a 2.8g zirconia-ceramic band that records speech on a deliberate double-tap — vibration and LED confirm it's recording — then the app turns it into notes, tasks, and reminders. Claimed noise-reducing mic with 3–5m pickup, offline storage with auto-sync, BLE 5.0 + NFC. The pitch is compelling: the deliberate double-tap (not always-listening) is the right privacy posture for a voice device, and the founder's stated ADHD-user angle — catching fleeting ideas away from a screen — is real friction. $159 Super Early Bird with no mandatory subscription is aggressive against Plaud-style recorders.

Who it's for (if the questions get answered)Note-takers, ADHD brains, anyone whose best ideas arrive in the shower and die before they reach a phone.
Campaign statusLIVE on Kickstarter since Sept 17, 2026. December 2026 shipping is the campaign's stated hope, not a promise.

Verified specifications

Build2.8g zirconia ceramic, under 8mm wide, IP68
ActivationDouble-tap with vibration + LED confirmation
MicrophoneNoise-reducing mic (3–5m pickup claimed)
StorageOffline storage with auto-sync
ConnectivityBLE 5.0 + NFC
Charging30-minute full charge
Campaign$159 Super Early Bird on Kickstarter; campaign opened Sept 17, 2026; shipping slated December 2026 in the launch release
Pros and cons

Pros

  • $159 with no subscription undercuts every AI voice recorder on the market by a mile.
  • The deliberate double-tap (not always-listening) is exactly the right design for a voice device.
  • Real friction, real audience: fleeting ideas away from a screen is a problem worth solving.

Cons

  • UNRESOLVED PRIVACY CONTRADICTION: marketing says transcription happens locally and audio is "never uploaded," but the iOS App Store listing says cloud processing and internet are required for transcription and AI features (runtimewire). Until Spark resolves this in writing, treat the privacy claims as disputed.
  • Conflicting battery specs: 8h recording / 30-min charge in the press release vs 6h / 1-hour charge on the about page.
  • Shipping is stated as December 2026 in the launch release but November 2026 in pre-launch materials — and Charley Tang has no prior shipped hardware products documented, promising 3-month campaign-to-shipping with zero independent reviews.
Verdict

Watch, don't back. The idea is great and the price is aggressive, but a voice recorder with an unresolved privacy contradiction doesn't get a recommendation. Revisit after the privacy conflict is resolved in writing and real units exist.

Circular Ring 3 Slim — “the payments bet”

Watch and wait · Price and date unconfirmed

The ultra-slim French ring announced at IFA 2026 with the one feature nobody else has: contactless payments from your finger.

What it does / key features

Circular's Ring 3 Slim (announced at IFA 2026, Sept 2–3) is the payments play this category was missing — NFC tap-to-pay with supported Visa and Mastercard cards, built into a properly slim ring — 5.9mm wide, 2.35mm thick, 1.9–3g, with a titanium outer shell over an epoxy resin interior. Add a haptic vibration engine (silent alarms, reminders) and the AI "Kira" app insights, plus the full health suite: heart rate, HRV, SpO2, respiration, skin temperature, sleep, activity, stress, women's health. Up to 10-day claimed battery; the charging case holds ~9–10 charges (~30 minutes per charge); 50m water resistance; sizes 6–14. Core features are subscription-free (blood-pressure, ECG, and glucose are hardware features of the separate Ring 3 Pro model — no paid subscription tier has been announced).

Who it's forPayment minimalists — people who want to leave the phone at home on a run and still buy coffee. And anyone who wants the slimmest ring in the category.
StatusPrice and launch date not announced; pre-orders "in the coming months," expected early 2027.

The subscription math (why crowdfunding rings keep winning)

Oura Ring 4 starts at $349 — and then charges $5.99/month. Over three years that's $349 + ~$216 = ~$565. The two top picks here are $349, flat, forever. The crowdfunding lane didn't just make rings cheaper to launch — it made them cheaper to own. When you compare any ring, price the three-year cost, not the box price. That's the entire reason this category grew up on Kickstarter: the incumbent's business model was the market gap.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a subscription for any of these?

No. Every pick here is subscription-free for its core features. That's the point of this roundup. Ultrahuman's optional PowerPlugs and Circular's Pro add-ons monetize extras, not the basics.

Are smart rings accurate?

Good enough for trends, not for medicine. Heart rate and sleep staging are solid across the established brands; anything blood-pressure-shaped (RingConn's vascular trends) is patterns, not diagnosis. Treat every metric as a trend line, not a lab result.

Is it safe to back a smart ring on Kickstarter?

It's never safe; it's a bet. Check the campaign's track record (Charley Tang's background is in consulting and AI-agent evaluation at ByteDance — no prior shipped hardware products are documented; KiWear has hands-on reviewers vouching for prototypes), read the risks section, and never pledge money you'd miss. Our WATCH picks above are flagged precisely because the bet isn't good enough yet.

What about Oura and Samsung Galaxy Ring?

They're the incumbents: Oura Ring 4 is excellent hardware with a $5.99/month tax; Galaxy Ring ($399) is Android-only (deepest features reserved for Samsung phones). This guide is about the crowdfunding lane — the rebels that don't charge rent. If you're deep in Samsung's ecosystem, the Galaxy Ring is the safe alternative to everything here.
